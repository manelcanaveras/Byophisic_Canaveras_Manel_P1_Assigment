This document explains the biological and structural significance of the outputs generated by the parsing scripts applied to the Ubiquitin structure (1UBQ).

Exercise 1: CA-CA Contacts (4.5 A threshold)
The script identified 79 CA-CA contacts. A 4.5 A threshold captures the immediate spatial proximity between adjacent alpha-carbons. This high number of contacts within a 76-residue protein reflects the tight packing of the hydrophobic core and the compact folded state of ubiquitin.

Exercise 2: Specific Residue Extraction (GLU 24)
The extraction successfully isolated the 9 heavy atoms of Glutamic Acid 24. By separating backbone atoms (N, CA, C, O) from side-chain atoms, this output demonstrates how specific molecular coordinates can be retrieved to analyze local residue environments and side-chain orientations.

Exercise 3: Potential Hydrogen Bonds (3.5 A threshold)
The output shows 142 potential hydrogen bonds among polar elements (O, N, S). This extensive dipole-dipole interaction network is structurally consistent with ubiquitin's highly stable secondary structure, which consists of a five-strand beta-sheet and a single alpha-helix.

Exercise 4: Residue Type Mapping (GLU)
The script mapped the spatial distribution of the 6 native Glutamic Acid residues in ubiquitin. Locating specific residue types is crucial for identifying functionally important regions, such as surface-exposed acidic patches that might be involved in protein-protein interactions.

Exercise 5: Peptide Bond Mapping (2.5 A threshold)
The script found exactly 75 C-N peptide bonds. Since ubiquitin is a continuous polypeptide chain of 76 amino acids, it must theoretically contain exactly N-1 peptide bonds. This result validates the structural integrity of the parsed backbone trace.

Exercise 6: Disulfide Bridges (3.0 A threshold)
The output correctly identified 0 disulfide (S-S) bridges. Ubiquitin lacks Cysteine (CYS) residues in its primary sequence. This confirms that the protein's tertiary structure is maintained entirely by non-covalent interactions rather than covalent cross-links.

Exercise 7: Inter-Residue Distance Matrix (GLY 10 & LYS 11)
The script calculated 36 pairwise distances between the 4 heavy atoms of Glycine and the 9 heavy atoms of Lysine. This complete Cartesian cross-product is a fundamental calculation in structural bioinformatics for identifying steric clashes or specific inter-residue contacts.

Exercise 8: Automated PDB Fetching
The script successfully connected to the RCSB PDB server and loaded the 1UBQ structure containing 660 atoms. Programmatic retrieval of structural data is essential for automating large-scale bioinformatics pipelines without manual file downloading.
